# Does an independent view check protect dense RGB depth corrections?

The earlier per-view scale experiment found that eight-pixel neighborhoods around fitting-fold feature matches covered 12.77% of valid pixels but only 4.69% of the remaining squared error. Test a dense source of evidence on the same twelve development conditions and nine RGB batches. All computation for this experiment is local. The following settings are fixed before opening any dense results for the new method.

## Prediction

Keep the frozen camera poses and previously selected focal lengths. At each target pixel consider its frozen axial depth multiplied by the exponential of 0, −0.04, +0.04, −0.08 or +0.08. Use identity first to break ties. Warp the four other RGB images using the corresponding pinhole projections. Rank those views by increasing camera-center distance, breaking ties by view index. Fit on ranks one and three and check on ranks two and four. These are disjoint source images observing the same target, so agreement across the two groups does not establish scene independence.

Represent each RGB channel by its difference from a 9×9 local mean. Use bilinear sampling, clamp each absolute channel discrepancy at 0.2 and average over channels. For a given target-source pair compare all five hypotheses on exactly the same pixels. Require finite positive depth, in-frame sampling with a full bilinear footprint and a projected depth no greater than 1.1 times the sampled frozen source depth for all candidates. Aggregate each candidate's cost in a 5×5 target patch. Require the center and at least 15 of 25 samples to be valid. Both source views must support the patch in their respective group. Equal weight each source. Use no reference map, valid-depth mask or sparse matches during prediction.

Compare three rules. The two-source fit selects the minimum cost from its fitting group. The four-source fit selects the minimum combined mean cost across all four sources. Both return identity when unsupported. The guarded rule reuses the two-source proposal and accepts it only when the validation group is supported and both fitting and validation mean costs decrease by at least 5% against identity, with a baseline cost greater than 1e-6 in each group. There is no filling across unsupported pixels. Preserve the three discrete correction maps, input and predicted-point hashes, counts per view, and processing times. Confirm on synthetic textured planes that the warps recover a known multiplicative error, that constant texture returns identity, and that missing validation rejects the proposal.

## Evaluation and decision

Seal predictions before scoring them against either reference. Use the established Umeyama, rigid ICP and nearest-neighbor evaluator. Normalize accuracy and completeness to local frozen FP32 per condition, and give all twelve conditions equal weight. Report both components, normal consistency, scene-wise results and the released-depth sensitivity check. Compare the guarded output with the best aggregate local control among frozen, direct focal, the previous shared and guarded depth scales, and the two- and four-source fits. Include the historical frozen RTX 3090 native and all three 40-update TCO arms. Advancement requires at least a 5% reduction against both strongest comparator groups on the registered score, neither distance component over 2% worse than the best local control, no scene worse than that control, and released-depth score no higher than that control.

For mechanism testing, apply the direct-focal global similarity to every dense candidate. Within and outside the original eight-pixel fitting-track neighborhoods report changed-pixel coverage, change in summed corresponding-pixel squared error, and the fraction of modified pixels with lower error. Keep these diagnostics separate from the registered surface decision. Report the fraction of all valid pixels on which an oracle with reference access could improve by choosing among the same five depth candidates and its attainable same-pixel RMSE under that fixed alignment. This reveals whether unhelpful corrections reflect poor visual selection or limited candidate range. The development test cannot establish generalization or a cross-device latency advantage.
