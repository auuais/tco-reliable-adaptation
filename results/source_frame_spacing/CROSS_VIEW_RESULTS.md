# Can another predicted view verify an update?

On the offset-25 development window, support from two of the other four frozen views covered 7.0% of sampled pixels on Heads, 52.5% on Chess and 50.6% on Fire. All three previously proposed maps increased distance to the fixed source surfaces in every scene. The registered selector retained direct focal in every case. A separate correction intersected the query ray with two nearby tangent planes, updating 2.8%, 32.0% and 35.4% of full-resolution pixels respectively. It reduced axial squared log error by 0.12% on average. Its registered whole-surface score rose from 0.97941 to 0.98745, with small regressions in every scene. The released score rose from 0.98430 to 0.99281. Both rules failed the registered surface criterion. [Protocol](../../docs/FROZEN_CROSS_VIEW_SUPPORT.md), [frozen support and decisions](cross_view_surface_predictions.json), [axial comparison](cross_view_axial.json) and [canonical scores](cross_view_surface_scores.json).

We also tested cross-view agreement as an update check on all 37 archived V6 TCO executions, using their original frames 0, 200, 400, 600 and 800. Initial two-view coverage was 17.5% on Heads, 58.3% on Chess and 56.0% on Fire. The rule accepted five updates, all on Heads; four were harmful. It rejected 32 updates including 22 with lower combined reconstruction error than initialization. Harm refers to either component worsening by more than the V6 threshold; this can coincide with an improved combined score. Across all runs, agreement change had rank correlation -0.025 with combined error. On repeat zero, all four accepted runs were harmful and none improved the combined score. Selecting via this rule yielded mean normalized error 1.01233 across all 37 executions and 1.01773 on the 29 repeat-zero executions; retaining the initial reconstruction gives 1.0. These descriptive results mix several calibration conditions on three scenes. [Acceptance protocol](../../docs/TCO_CROSS_VIEW_ACCEPTANCE.md) and [all observations, decisions and per-scene comparisons](tco_cross_view_acceptance.json).

Cross-view agreement has inadequate value as a stand-alone acceptance signal here. A different local test varies the model's reference image while keeping the physical views fixed, measuring whether this induces reproducible errors that can be reduced by aggregating predictions.
