## 1. Decision this study should support

Measure whether the first optimizer step proposed by the existing five-scale LoRA training loss lowers held-out sparse depth error after MASt3R recomputes image matches and aligns the scene. Separate the sign of the local slope from the outcome of actually taking the step. A step can initially point downhill and still overshoot. The result is a diagnostic on new development rooms. An accuracy claim on independent scenes or dense surfaces would need another study.

The parent release is `54abf8c70252f965bc813dae329e74bab05a8965`. Its calibration-compensation candidate raised held-source MAE by 0.192% in one temporal direction and lowered it by 0.012% in the other. A control that used the same forward calculation and detached the scale gradient beat the candidate in both directions. Those experiments make the direction of the training objective worth checking. ## 3. Direction, endpoint and controls

1. **Start from the pristine model in each room and temporal direction.** Reuse P3, the previously implemented sensor-only five-scale surrogate with differentiation through the solve, training graph weight zero and spatial support fold 0 for step 0. Use the parent’s adapter placement, rank 4, alpha 1, seed 42, deterministic precision policy, all 20 directed image pairs, gradient clipping at 1 and fresh Adam at learning rate 0.0001. Keep the backbone and buffers frozen. Bind the exact model, code, optimizer defaults and precision settings in the manifest.
2. **Measure the real update.** Save named adapter factors before and after exactly one optimizer step and retain the unclipped gradient. Define the direction `d = theta_after - theta_before` in factor coordinates. For each signed multiplier `s`, restore the same initial state and evaluate `theta(s) = theta_before + s*d`. Use the saved factor tensors at 0 and +1 and verify reconstruction at these two points to recorded dtype precision. At other multipliers form the difference in float64 before casting to the registered parameter dtype. Record actual displacements, hashes and the effective change to the adapted matrices. No optimizer step is taken from a perturbed endpoint. A zero/nonfinite direction or a changed backbone is an engineering failure.
3. **Measure the training prediction of the direction.** Log the analytic inner product of the pre-clip gradient with `d`, and the actual surrogate loss `J(s)` at `s = -1, -0.5, 0, +0.5, +1` on the **same allowed fold**. Freeze baseline confidence, observations, folds, gauges and normalizers as in P3. Use the same loss arithmetic that produced the update. Optionally log a separately labeled FP32 shadow diagnostic, fixed before registration. An apparent descent in a surrogate that differs from the executed objective cannot determine the verdict.
4. **Run a fresh native endpoint at each signed multiplier.** Recompute full FP32 pair inference, fresh matches, confidences, native coarse/fine alignment (300/300) and the same calibrated camera/depth/pose priors (weight 0.01) using only permitted inputs. Give each state an empty cache and repeatable initialization. Keep the five views, camera conventions, settings and model fixed across signed states. Record the graph edge counts and correspondence fingerprints, and the selected tree if available. Native matching and the median gauge can change discretely, so the measured slope is a finite-scale observation.
5. **Fix the unit convention.** For each state, compute one global scale `g0(s)` as the pooled midpoint median of allowed measured axial depth divided by native predicted axial depth at the same pixels. The primary prediction in meters is `g0(s) * native_depth(s)`. Perform this conversion exactly once. Use no fitted per-view depth scale in the primary endpoint. Record `g0(s)` and raw depth units. A separately labeled constant-`g0(0)` sensitivity may be computed from the same sealed native arrays to identify gauge effects. It cannot change the primary decision.
6. **Measure numerical repeatability.** Per room and side, run independent fresh-process, empty-cache repeats at 0 and +1. The inventory is seven native endpoints per case (five unique signed states and two repeats), six cases, **42 native endpoints and six one-step directions**. The repeated +1 endpoint uses the saved identical factor state. These repeats measure endpoint variability. They do not estimate between-room uncertainty.

## 4. Scoring and decision rules to freeze before launch

For each view, average absolute axial depth error on its fixed held mask in millimeters. Average the five views equally to obtain `E(s)` per room and direction. Report every view’s count and mean, every room and side, and equal-room directional means. Use the first 0 and +1 captures for the primary effects; reserve repeats for noise estimation. Also calculate native error on the permitted adaptation-query coordinates, with separate labeling because that query helped choose the update. It helps localize a discrepancy between training loss, native alignment and generalization to a new temporal source.

| Quantity | Definition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Central slope at each scale | `D_h = [E(+h) - E(-h)] / (2h)`, for `h=0.5,1` | A negative sign favors the proposed direction over its mirror |
| Realized error change | `A = E(+1) - E(0)` | A negative sign means the actual optimizer step improved held depth |
| Relative gain | `100 * [E(0) - E(+1)] / E(0)` | Effect size comparable across rooms |
| Repeat deadband per case | `b = max(0.05 mm, 2*abs(E(0) - E_repeat(0)), 2*abs(E(+1) - E_repeat(+1)))` | Minimum interpretable absolute difference under this protocol |

The 0.05 mm floor is an engineering decision threshold on the **average** metric, not an estimate of sensor accuracy. Report the underlying continuous values including small effects. Apply the deadband to the **numerator** of each central difference, and to `A`. Require `grad(J)·d < 0` and `J(+1) < J(0)` for an unambiguous surrogate descent. Report numerical ties and same-state surrogate repeat differences.

- **Aligned and beneficial case:** the surrogate descends, both central differences favor +d beyond the deadband, and the realized full step lowers error beyond the deadband.
- **Opposed case:** the surrogate descends, both central differences favor −d beyond the deadband, and the full step increases error beyond the deadband.
- **Other outcomes:** preserve the actual signs and reasons. A downhill local slope and harmful full step suggests a step-size issue; disagreement across scales suggests curvature, matching changes or numerical sensitivity. If the surrogate itself fails to descend, classify the case as uninformative about surrogate-to-endpoint agreement.
- **Study verdict:** call a directional trend reproducible on this development screen only when at least two of three rooms have the same decisive class in **both** temporal directions, the third room has no decisively contrary case, and all six cases are valid. Otherwise report mixed or indeterminate evidence. Always report how many full steps actually helped, worsened or fell within the deadband, even when the slope classification is mixed. Pixels, views and both temporal directions within one room are not independent scene replicates.

If the direction is repeatedly opposed on held data, inspect the prespecified adaptation-query diagnostic. Worsening native error on both sources while the training loss falls supports investigating an alignment-aware objective. Improvement on the adaptation query and deterioration on held depth points toward temporal generalization or overfitting. Repeatedly beneficial directions with marginal total gains shift attention to step magnitude or later trajectory. These are interpretations for a subsequent decision. This goal ends with the directional evidence and verdict.
