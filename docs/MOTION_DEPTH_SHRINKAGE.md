# How much of the triangulation error comes from the size of the update?

Use the already sealed fifteen short-spacing estimates, natural original target depth, common scene scale and valid-depth mask. Keep every target, source and eligibility decision fixed. This follow-up describes the estimator that failed its registered feasibility test. Its results cannot overturn that decision or permit transfer spending.

For each valid target pixel define e = log(original depth / measured depth). Set u to the RGB-motion correction in log depth, clipped to ±0.08 on eligible pixels and zero elsewhere. A scalar update of size a has squared error sum C + 2aB + a²A, with C = sum(e²), B = sum(eu), and A = sum(u²). Compare the a = 0 and a = 1 endpoints against the archived natural scores before any fitting. Minimize the mean per-target relative squared error, weighting each of the 15 targets equally. Fit a in [0,1]. Report the normalized first derivative at zero, the stock unit step, the optimal unrestricted signed scalar, and the best achievable improvement with a nonnegative scalar. A negative derivative at zero means small positive updates initially help. Negative fitted signs only diagnose a direction error.

For each of the frozen/measured camera and any-source/two-sided combinations, report pooled fits, per-scene oracle fits and leave-one-scene-out step sizes. The latter fits a shared scalar on ten targets and scores the remaining five without their labels. Also report the upper bound obtained by fitting each individual target's scalar to its own truth. The primary diagnosis concerns frozen cameras with two-sided evidence. Preserve camera regimes as separate analyses, including their different observable areas. These post-hoc depth-error diagnostics use known development labels, three scenes and zero additional cloud budget.
