# TCO head readouts on two real CPU images

The pinned TCO backbone concatenates frame and global features at all 24 layers. In our camera-and-depth configuration the depth head reads layers 4, 11, 17 and 23, and the camera head reads the last layer. Retaining those four concatenations reduced the output list's tensor storage from **20,840,448 to 3,473,408 bytes** on two 84 × 112 images. The CPU control used the pretrained VGGT checkpoint, FP32 arithmetic, gradient checkpointing, and disabled tracking and point heads. [Upstream TCO aggregator](https://github.com/cvlab-stonybrook/TCO/blob/65387333877723cf99086f7f29089379dd50c59a/tco_vggt_lora.py) · [Code change](../../patches/tco-selected-readouts.patch)

All four consumed feature tensors, the pose encoding, dense depth, depth confidence and the gradient with respect to the input patch tokens were **bitwise identical** between configurations. The scalar test objective was the sum of mean squared pose encoding, mean depth and mean log confidence. It exercised both output heads and backpropagation. [Raw control, weight and tensor hashes](real_cpu_control.json) · [Test implementation](../../scripts/check_selected_readouts_cpu.py)

For five 518 × 392 images with 1,041 tokens per view and 2,048 channels per concatenation, omitting 20 concatenations corresponds to 406.6 MiB of BF16 or 813.3 MiB of FP32 output-list storage. These are tensor-size calculations. Device peak allocation, precision effects during full CUDA TCO and wall-clock performance require matched GPU measurements. The original twelve-run calibration experiment uses its frozen configuration.
