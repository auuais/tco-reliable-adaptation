## The primary comparison

| Arm | Real Adam updates | Parameters updated per scene | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|---|
| L10 | 10 | Standard rank-4 decoder LoRA | Equal-update comparison |
| LN10 | 10 | Identical LoRA plus the four final global LayerNorm tensors | Candidate |
| L40 | 40 | Identical standard LoRA | Full-length quality and latency reference |

Run all three arms on the fresh roster once engineering and data checks pass, including when early development geometry looks poor. On confirmation, answer three questions separately. Does LN10 improve mean surface accuracy over L10 while preserving completeness within 3%? Does LN10 stay within **+1% mean surface accuracy error and +3% mean surface completeness error** of L40? Does LN10 reduce synchronized end-to-end scene time versus L40? All three decisions must pass for the full claim. “Better at ten steps” in this study means an accuracy improvement under the stated completeness constraint. A claim that *both* errors improve requires separate evidence for both. Report any confirmed tradeoffs.

The mechanism is concrete. A LayerNorm applies `γ ⊙ z + β` to normalized features `z`. In the last global block, changing γ reweights existing feature channels across views, and changing β shifts their offsets. For a following linear map W, the scale change contributes `W diag(Δγ) z`. This offers a different set of weight updates from a rank-4 additive correction. A lower adaptation loss alone cannot establish better reconstructed geometry.
