# Can RGB matches see the remaining within-view error?

Use the nine existing direct-focal point maps and two registered depth references. Compare each prediction and its reference at the same valid pixel after independently aligning each view by a proper similarity. This removes camera rigid motion and one scale per view at evaluation time. The squared residual is the same quantity used in the independent-similarity row of the geometry diagnostic. All group masks involving reference geometry are evaluation-only.

Measure the share of valid pixels and the share of residual squared error in three disjoint regions. First mark pixels within two pixels (Euclidean distance) of invalid depth inside the raster as missing-depth neighbors. Next locate a geometric jump wherever two valid horizontal or vertical neighbors are separated by more than 0.05 m in the reference point map. Mark both sides of the jump and their two-pixel neighborhood, excluding the first region. The remaining valid pixels form the interior. A geometric jump may also represent a steep surface. Image borders alone do not count as missing depth.

Within each region measure distance to the nearest detected RGB feature participating in the fitting fold. Round subpixel coordinates to the nearest raster location and count pixels at most eight pixels from one of those locations as covered. Report the same calculation for the union of the two folds as an upper estimate of the observed feature coverage. Keep masks fixed, show scene-level and both-reference results, and average twelve condition-level fractions equally. Sum groups before dividing to compute a conditional RMSE. Check summed counts and squared errors against the published per-view similarity optima. No model tuning, depth supervision at inference or additional rental is involved.
