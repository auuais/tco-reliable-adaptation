## A. Give cached adaptation one early correction

**Why this comparison matters.** The published cached ten-step method cut time by 29–32% versus standard ten-step TCO on transfer, but missed both quality margins. The approximately 4× and 23.6% figures use full 40 as the denominator. Ordinary four/five-step TCO also had better average geometry at similar time. Earlier work already tested a static 15/20-step sweep and a boundary adapter with own-feature and shuffled controls. The new intervention changes **when** earlier stages can update. [Original measurements](../results/tco_one_percent_v1/README.md)

| Arm | LoRA updates | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Full10 / Full40 | All 24 stages every step | Equal-update and full-quality references |
| Cache10 | Last 12 stages on all 10 steps with frozen features reused | Existing fast baseline |
| Refresh1 | All 24 at step 1, last 12 only on steps 2–10 | Main new candidate |
| Refresh1 uncached | Identical weight and Adam schedule, recompute early features every time | Isolate cache reuse from changing which weights move |
| Refresh2, conditional | All 24 at steps 1 and 6, suffix only otherwise; rebuild after both | Test whether the correction must be renewed |
